When difficult messages do not land

A practical approach to career engagement using SJS expectations.

Case study 3

The case in one minute

Meet Susie, 18 months into a Manager role.

What Susie brings

  • Dedicated and committed
  • Strong technical knowledge
  • Well-liked by colleagues
  • Reliable in routine operations
  • Positive attitude towards learning

Where the role fit breaks down

  • Not yet independent at grade
  • Needs frequent direction and reassurance
  • Struggles with strategy and prioritisation
  • Low confidence in leading and influencing
  • Support has not produced lasting change
The warning signal

After 18 months, Susie still relies on the RO for decisions and does not know that regrading is being considered.

The message was softened until it was no longer clear. The problem is not the existence of a difficult message. It is the gap between what the RO intended and what Susie understood.

Diagnose

Why the message did not land

Four things went wrong, in order of how often they happen.

1

Hints replaced clarity

Development feedback was phrased too gently, so the seriousness was lost.

2

Support was mistaken for progress

Training and reduced workload continued, but the gap was not reframed explicitly.

3

No shared reference point

The discussion was not visibly anchored to grade-level role expectations.

4

No understanding check

The RO did not ask Susie to explain what the feedback meant for the role.

Kindness without clarity can create false reassurance.
Anchor on expectations

Use SJS as the common language

Shift from personal judgement to role alignment.

  1. 1Identify the relevant grade-level SJS expectations.
  2. 2Compare expectations with observable work examples.
  3. 3Describe the consistency and independence required at grade.
  4. 4Agree on what meeting the role looks like in practice.

“This is not about your worth. It is about whether the current work pattern consistently meets the scope and independence expected at this grade.”

SJS supports consistency, but judgement should still consider context, evidence and the person’s circumstances.

Engage with clarity and care

A six-step conversation flow

Follow the order. Each step makes the next one easier to hear.

1

Set intent

“I want an honest discussion about role expectations and your career.”

2

Recognise strengths

Acknowledge contribution without using praise to hide the message.

3

Show the evidence

Connect SJS expectations to specific, repeated work examples.

4

State the message

Say clearly that current performance is not consistently at grade.

5

Pause and listen

Invite response, acknowledge impact and check understanding.

6

Agree the path

Explain decisions, support, next steps and career options.

Suggested dialogue

What can the RO actually say?

Wording you can adapt. Keep the meaning, change the voice.

Clear and respectful
“Susie, I value your commitment, technical knowledge and reliability. I also need to be clear about the Manager role.”
“Based on the SJS expectations and the work examples we have reviewed, you are not yet consistently performing independently at the Manager grade.”
“Despite the support provided, this gap has not become sustainable. Management and HR have therefore decided to regrade the role.”
Then preserve dignity and agency
“I recognise this is difficult news, and I want to give you time to respond.”
“This decision does not erase your strengths or contribution.”
“Let us discuss where your strengths can be best applied, what support is available, and what a successful career path could look like from here.”
Listen and check understanding

Questions that turn the talk into engagement

Six prompts for the pause after the message.

Reflect

How do you assess your performance against the Manager SJS?

Explore

Which parts of the role energise you, and which require sustained support?

Surface

What feedback have you heard repeatedly over the past year?

Understand

What is your understanding of the message I have shared today?

Career

Would a specialist, technical or project pathway better use your strengths?

Support

What support would help you transition successfully from here?

Do not ask questions that reopen a decision already made. Be transparent about what is decided and what remains open for discussion.
Key takeaway

Close with truth, dignity and a path forward

The conversation succeeds when Susie leaves with:

  • A clear understanding of the performance gap
  • The decision and its rationale
  • Recognition of strengths and contribution
  • A defined support and transition plan
  • A credible view of future career options

Kindness is not avoiding the difficult message. Kindness is delivering it clearly, respectfully and early enough for the staff member to respond.
